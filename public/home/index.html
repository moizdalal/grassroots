How it works

Four steps. Nothing weird.

Scope the work, pick a bid, pay, approve the result. No recruiter calls. No agency intake forms.

/ 04

Post a project

Write a short brief. Set a budget range. Pick one-off, phased, or retainer. Takes about five minutes.

Get scoped bids

Local students send a short pitch and a price. Read them at your own pace. No recruiter calls.

Pay, we hold it

Pick a bid. Fund the agreed amount through Stripe. It sits there, untouched, until you say so.

Release on approval

Review the work. Ask for changes if needed. Release funds when it's yours. The student gets paid same day.

Questions

Things people ask before posting.

How does pricing work?

Students bid their own price on each project; you pick the bid you like. Businesses pay a 10% platform fee on top of the bid, shown before you pay. It's waived during launch.

What kinds of students are on Grassroots?

College students at four-year and two-year schools, mostly juniors and seniors. School email verification is coming. Each profile lists school, major, and past work. You can filter by skill, school, or city before you ever post.

What happens if I don't approve the work?

Report a problem from the engagement page and we lock the payment. We review the work against the agreed scope. Then we release it, have the student redo it, or refund you. See the refund policy.

Can I hire the same student again?

Yes. You can re-post directly to a past consultant, set up a retainer contract, or open a new public listing and invite them to bid. Most repeat posters lock in a retainer after two projects.

Are taxes and 1099s handled?

Payouts go to students through Stripe. Grassroots issues tax forms to students where the law requires it. You'll get a clean year-end summary of what you paid through the platform for your own books.

What cities are you in?

We launched in 14 college towns across Florida, Georgia, and the Carolinas. New towns open when there's a critical mass of students and businesses; you can request your city from the footer.
