Chapter I. mission

The mission, in one paragraph.

A small business should not have to write a four-thousand-dollar check for a logo, or wait six weeks for a one-page website. A college student should not graduate with a 0-line resume just because nobody local had the budget for an intern. Grassroots is the bridge. We pair local students with the small businesses in their town, scope the work, hold the payment, and release it when the work ships. That's the entire thing. We're not disrupting anybody. We're just doing the obvious thing the agencies forgot to do.

— The field office
Chapter II. From the founder

A letter, unfolded.

From the desk of Wes Holloway · founder
4th floor, the chestnut, Gainesville, FL
April 14, 2026
Re: who we are
Folio 02 / 03

Dear reader —

In August of 2025 I watched my aunt Cherie write a check for $4,400 to a creative agency three towns over. She'd been quoted a logo, a one-pager, and a "brand voice document." Twelve weeks later she got a logo. Just the logo. No site. No voice doc. It cost more than the new oven she'd been saving for.

I was a junior at the time. I had two friends who could've done that work in a weekend for $300. They were both delivering DoorDash. The mismatch wouldn't leave me alone.

So I started writing it down. The shape of the problem was simple: small businesses needed light, scoped consulting work — logos, decks, copy, photos, Shopify cleanups — and there was nowhere honest to find it. Agencies wanted retainers. Freelance sites wanted volume. Nobody wanted $240 worth of work, paid like a contract, done by somebody you'd recognize at the farmer's market.

Meanwhile, the talent was sitting in the same zip code. Sophomores with portfolios. Seniors with case studies. Twenty-year-olds who had already shipped more work for free than most agencies bill for in a quarter. They needed money. They needed real reviews. They needed a way to point at something and say I shipped that.

We launched a prototype with three students and one café in October. We held $180 for the café, the menu shipped on a Sunday, we released the funds on a Monday. The café tipped extra. The student told two friends. The week after, we had eight more businesses asking.

That's the entire thing. We hold the money where you can see it. We don't take a fee until the work funds. We let students bid honest prices instead of bidding against ninety strangers in Bangalore. We review every release. We send the receipts.

If you are a small business: post a project. If you are a student: apply, or refer three friends and skip the line. If you are neither — just send us a letter. The post box at the bottom of this page is real.

Yours, in the field —
Wes Holloway founder · junior, marketing · University of Florida

Chapter III. An origin story

Nine entries, in chronological order.

Aug · 2025

The $4,400 check.

Wes watches his aunt sign for a logo she could've gotten anywhere for a fraction. Writes the first note in the margin of his marketing textbook: "the market is broken for sub-$1k work."

The problem · located
Sep · 2025

A coffee, three students, one idea.

First whiteboard session at a coffee shop in Gainesville with two friends — Maya (design), Jordan (research). They sketch a marketplace where students bid on local briefs. Napkin still on file.

Oct · 2025

Side Street Coffee, $180.

First paid engagement on a Google Form. Menu redesign for Side Street Coffee. Funds held in a Stripe test account. Released on a Monday. Owner tipped an extra $30.

First funded · v0
Nov · 2025

Grassroots, the real site.

Real domain, real payments, real reviews. 14 students joined from UF and Santa Fe. 8 local businesses post listings in the first week.

Dec · 2025

The six-dimension review.

After a confused star-rating fight, we replace the 1-to-5 with six dimensions — work, communication, timeliness, professionalism, collaboration, value. Owners say it's "what a recruiter would actually read."

Feb · 2026

Project number 100.

A market research deck for Split/Fix. The team prints the receipt, frames it, hangs it on the office wall. (It's still there.)

100 funded engagements
Mar · 2026

Five more towns.

Gainesville, Tallahassee, Athens, Charleston, Chapel Hill. Each town opened by three founding students. The pattern holds: cafés first, then bike shops, then florists, then everyone else.

Apr · 2026

Invite-only · the badge gets valuable.

Acceptance falls to ~12%. We add the referral path: bring three businesses to the platform and you skip the queue. Half of the next cohort gets in this way.

Today

14 towns. $340k paid to students. And counting.

Chapter IV. The credo

Six things we believe.

01

Local is not a feature. It is the product.

Every other platform tries to deliver "anywhere, anytime." We deliver here, this week, with a person you can wave at. Local is the entire reason this works.

02

Money waits in the open.

No invoice chasing. No 30-day terms. Grassroots holds the dollars, processed by Stripe, where both sides can see them, and they only move when the work moves.

03

A receipt is a story.

Every closed engagement generates a real, signed, six-dimension review. We don't trust five-star averages. We trust paragraphs from people who paid.

04

A bid is not a quote.

Bids are pitches: a short note, a price, a plan. No mystery margins, no scope tricks, no "starting from." The number on the bid is the number on the receipt.

05

Reviews are for life.

The reviews attached to a profile travel with the student forever. Through internships, jobs, grad school. We don't gate them, expire them, or rewrite the past.

06

A network of three is enough.

If a student brings three businesses onto the platform, they've already proven they belong on it. That's the entire invite system. No algorithm.
